# Rabbit-People — Charter Decision Points

**Status:** `Proposed:` 2026-10-21. Produced by the first M-1 pass (the rabbit pilot). Nothing here is decided.

We tried to work out rabbit-people's bodies from real biology. Biology settled a few things and ruled out others, but
most of what shapes these people day to day can only be decided by you. This document lists those decisions, the most
far-reaching first, with what each option would change. Technical detail and sources are in
`01_Physiology_and_Life_History.md` (cited below as "L1 §n").

---

## Before you start

### What biology already settled (no decision needed)

- **They are a new species.** They can't have children with rabbits. They can't have children with humans either,
  unless you rule for an engineered exception (CD-12). (L1 Domain 0, O-1 and O-2)
- **They are much bigger than any rabbit.** Language needs a large brain, and the only kind we have data on is
  human-sized. A rabbit-sized body can't carry it. The largest rabbit relative that ever lived weighed about 8–12 kg.
  (L1 §1.1)
- **They talk (or sign) in a full language** and can take on their creators' culture. (L1 §13.1, §15.1)
- **At human size they can't breed like rabbits.** A month-long pregnancy, big litters every few weeks, and adulthood
  in four months only work in a 2 kg body. (L1 O-5; HWC-10)
- **Their babies get the mother's antibodies before birth,** whichever design you pick. (L1 §10.10)

### The D-7 rule in practice

D-7 says the animal side must always show in how they *behave*. **Biology doesn't force any rabbit behavior to
stay;** it only removes some of the breeding-rate traits at human size. So the animal side will exist only if your
choices keep it. Each option below is marked:

- **Animal side (D-7):** *strong* / *partial* / *none*: how much rabbit behavior it keeps.
- **Audience can read it:** *high* / *medium* / *low*: how visible that is on screen (D-7 point 5: among plausible
  options, the readable one is a legitimate preference).

If every decision took its human-type option, the race would fail D-7. Each lineage needs some animal-side options.
How many is your call. There's a quick-reference table at the end.

### Lineages

Every decision below **can differ between lineages** (one rabbit-people program by one set of creators: D-1, D-5),
unless noted. Where a difference would stop lineages from interbreeding, it says so.

## CD-1 · How big is an adult?

**The question.** What size range are adults? Biology sets a floor (see above). Above that, it's yours. Your rule that
every character is a real doll (C-2, Q-9) may already answer it.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Human range (about 50–80 kg)** | The worked numbers in L1 assume this. Big ears cool the body less well at this size. The rabbit gut trick of re-eating soft droppings loses its reason (CD-8). Rabbit breeding pace is ruled out (CD-2). **Animal side:** no direct effect. **Handwaves:** none new |
| **B. Smaller than human (about 30–45 kg)** | Surprisingly little changes in how fast they grow, breed, and age: the predictions barely move between 30 and 80 kg. The brain is a bigger share of the body, so feeding it is a sharper question if they're plant-eaters (CD-8). Whether a smaller brain could still handle language is unknown (a research item). **Animal side:** no direct effect |
| **C. Larger than human (over 80 kg)** | Real mammals at this size still fit plausible ranges. Shedding heat gets harder, especially with fur (CD-9). **Animal side:** no direct effect |

**Knock-ons.** If lineages of different sizes interbreed, the mother's size limits the baby's size, so crosses would
work better one way round than the other. **Can differ by lineage:** yes.

## CD-2 · How fast do they grow up, have children, and age?

**The question.** Every body runs on one built-in speed dial for development. It sets pregnancy length, how helpless
newborns are, the age at adulthood, and lifespan, **all together.** You pick one setting. You can't mix rabbit-speed
breeding with human-length lives. **This matters more than size** for family life, generations, and how fast their
culture changes.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Rabbit pace, scaled up to their size** | Pregnancy about 1.5–2 months. Helpless newborns, born into a nest. Litters of about five are possible, but at most roughly one or two litters a year at full size. Adult in about a year. Maximum life about 20–27 years. Generations turn over several times faster than human ones, so their culture drifts faster per century. **Big problem:** the brain and a first language would have to develop within about a year, and nothing in the evidence shows that can work (pHWC-1, a **bend**). **Animal side:** strong (litters, nests, fast generations), if CD-3 keeps rabbit-type reproduction. **Audience can read it:** high |
| **B. A middle pace, off both parents' lines but inside what real mammals of this size do** | Illustrative only: pregnancy of a few months, one to three young, adulthood after a few years, life of several decades. The rule is that longer life comes with later adulthood and fewer young. The language and learning period fits more comfortably than in A. **Animal side:** partial (small litters and nest-building are possible). **Audience:** medium. **Handwaves:** HWC-13 (a cost: the trade-offs must be shown) |
| **C. Human pace** | Pregnancy about nine months, usually one baby, adulthood in the mid-teens, life of about seventy years or more. Childhood and language learning work as in humans. Fits a long upbringing inside a creator culture. **Animal side:** none from life history, so other decisions must carry it. **Audience:** low (life history is mostly invisible on screen) |

**Ruled out by biology:** rabbit pace without scaling (HWC-10); lives far beyond about five times what their size
predicts, or long lives combined with fast breeding (HWC-11). **Watch:** if their size was reached by speeding up
growth, expect earlier aging, like large dog breeds (HWC-14).

**Knock-ons.** Domains 10–12 and 15 (L1); how many generations pass between their creation and *New Centauri* (C-25;
Q-2's era); M-7's calibration of the culture methods. **Can differ by lineage:** yes, but whether lineages of different
pace could interbreed has no evidence either way.

## CD-3 · How do pregnancy, birth, and nursing work?

**The question.** Within the pace you chose, which reproductive machinery? The base animal has no monthly cycle and
ovulates in response to mating. It can conceive again right after giving birth. Late-pregnancy hormones drive a
nest-building sequence (digging, carrying bedding, pulling out its own fur to line the nest). It nurses for about three
minutes roughly once a day, with very rich milk, and weans the litter abruptly when the next pregnancy arrives. Humans
have a monthly cycle, nurse often over years, and rely on help from others.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Human-type** | Monthly cycle, frequent nursing, long care with help from others. **Animal side:** none from reproduction. **Audience:** low |
| **B. Rabbit-type machinery, sized to your CD-2 choice** | Mating-triggered ovulation (this works at any size). No monthly cycle; fertility depends on receptivity. A strong nest-building drive late in pregnancy. Brief, rare nursing: untested at human size (pHWC-2). Conceiving right after birth: energy-costly (pHWC-4). Litters, if CD-2 allows: whether a litter can be born through an upright pelvis is unresearched (pHWC-3). **Knock-ons:** newborns may need the mother's milk scent to find the nipple (CD-13); in the base, competition between females over nest sites sometimes leads to killing others' young (L1 §16.6); mother-only care doesn't fit a long childhood, so this pairs with CD-2 A or a fast B. **Animal side:** strong. **Audience:** high, but this is sexual and maternal behavior, so it's sensitive |
| **C. Mixed: rabbit-type ovulation, human-type nursing and upbringing** | Keeps the size-proof part: mating-triggered ovulation, no monthly cycle. Avoids the untested nursing pattern. Fits CD-2 B or C. **Animal side:** partial. **Audience:** low to medium (private, but it shapes relationships and family planning) |

**Also in this decision:** whether breeding is **seasonal** (in the base, day length and fresh green food switch
breeding on; constant artificial light switches the season off), and the **mating system** (the base lives in small
groups with separate pecking orders for males and females; who fathers which young is unstudied).

**Can differ by lineage:** yes.

## CD-4 · How much of the prey animal's fear-and-alarm system do they keep?

**The question.** The base animal's fear works differently from the human picture. Under threat, its heart *slows*
and it freezes. When seized, it can go limp (tonic immobility). It keeps two kinds of watch: one for predators, one for
its own kind. It shifts when it's active according to danger. It signals alarm by foot-thumping, flashing its tail when
its young are near, and screaming when caught. It shows less pain when watched. And a narrow window in early infancy
decides whether it fears a *category* of caretaker, without touching its fear of predators. Domestication shrank this
system but did not remove it. **This is probably the most readable animal trait available** (L1 §14, §15).

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Wild-type, full system** | **Animal side:** strong. **Audience:** very high (body language under stress). **Knock-ons:** stress spills into breeding (nesting delays and lost young in the base), pecking order, and possibly the gut. The infancy window makes the upbringing choice matter (CD-13). Gap: there's no matching human fear data yet, so comparisons stay rough (a research item) |
| **B. Domestic-type, reduced but present** | Calmer on average; more individuals toward the calm end. **Animal side:** strong to partial. **Audience:** high. If such a lineage later lives with real danger, free-living rabbits show selection pushing back toward the wild type. Pairs naturally with CD-5 B |
| **C. Human-type** | **Animal side:** none here. Removes the most readable animal trait, so D-7 must be met elsewhere |

**Note.** The pieces (freezing, going limp, the two kinds of watch, alarm signals, the infancy window) could in
principle be designed separately; whether that works biologically is untested. **Can differ by lineage:** yes. The
infancy window also creates differences between lineages raised by different caretakers (CD-13).

## CD-5 · Which rabbits did the creators start from?

**The question.** Wild rabbits from the species' native range in Spain and Portugal (two subspecies: a smaller
south-western one and a larger one), wild or feral rabbits from elsewhere, or domestic breeds? Until you choose,
domestication history isn't allowed to explain anything about the race (M-2's sequencing rule).

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Wild native stock** (and which subspecies) | Full-strength fear system; more night-active. The smaller subspecies has shorter ears and feet and a winter-to-spring breeding season. **Animal side:** strengthens CD-4 |
| **B. Domestic breeds** | Reduced fear system (smaller amygdala, larger prefrontal area). Size genes, including a dwarfing variant that kills when inherited from both parents. Floppy-ear variants in some breeds, which bring ear disease. More taste buds. Nursing earlier in the evening. **Animal side:** weakens CD-4 |
| **C. Mixed sources** | Founders carry both; later conditions sort them. Free-living rabbits select against domestic variants |

**Can differ by lineage:** yes; a natural source of lineage differences.

## CD-6 · Body shape: posture, legs, hands, tail

**The question.** An upright two-legged frame with human hands, or some of the rabbit's limb design? The rabbit's
design has long hind feet used on the toes, forearms locked palm-down that can't turn over, and a bounding gait.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Human frame and hands** (rabbit features from CD-7, plus an optional tail) | Hands for tools and for sign language. **Animal side:** comes from elsewhere. Matches dolls most easily (Q-9) |
| **B. Human frame with rabbit-style legs and feet** | A distinctive stance and stride. The load on joints and back is unresearched. **Animal side:** partial (movement). **Audience:** high |
| **C. Rabbit-style limbs throughout** | No rabbit relative ever came near this size, so there's no real model to follow. Hands can't turn palm-up, which limits handling and may rule out sign language, so the voice would have to carry
language (L1 coherence row CM-10). Likely excluded by the doll rule |

**Tail.** The tail-flash alarm signal exists only if a tail with a white underside is kept. **Foot-thumping** needs
suitable feet; whether others "hear" it through the ground is unstudied. **Can differ by lineage:** yes.

## CD-7 · The head: eyes, ears, nose, whiskers, voice

**The question.** Which sense organs come from which parent? The sub-choices:
- **Eyes:** side-facing with a near-panoramic view, attention tuned to scanning the horizon, and two color types; or
  forward-facing, with a sharp center and three color types.
- **Ears:** big sound-funnels that also shed heat, hearing up to about 49 kHz; or human ears, up to about 20 kHz.
  Either way they can hear speech.
- **Nose:** about 768 smell genes against about 400. More genes doesn't mean a better sense of smell.
- **Whiskers:** yes or no.
- **Voice box:** speech needs a human-type voice box. Without one, language has to be signed, which needs hands
  (CD-6).

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Human head with rabbit outer ears** | Readable, and ears can show stress if they're mobile (how mobile rabbit ears are is unstudied). The ears' cooling role shrinks at this size. **Animal side:** partial. **Audience:** high |
| **B. Rabbit sensory head** (side eyes and horizon-scanning attention, two-color vision, big ears, rabbit nose, whiskers; human-type voice box if they speak) | Where they look, how they startle, and how they scan all become rabbit-like. Fits a night clock (CD-11). **Animal side:** strong. **Audience:** very high |
| **C. Mixed: forward eyes for close work, plus rabbit ears, nose, and whiskers** | **Animal side:** partial. **Audience:** high |

**Can differ by lineage:** yes; very visible.

## CD-8 · What do they eat, and what gut and teeth go with it?

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Human omnivore gut and teeth** | **Animal side:** none here, unless babies still learn food preferences from the mother (below) |
| **B. A plant-eater gut redesigned for their size** | Fermenting gut without re-eating droppings. Teeth that grow all their lives and need tough, fibrous food. Many small meals; a hard minimum of fiber; high water needs. Needs a creator design item. Whether such a gut can fuel a language-sized brain is an open question (pHWC-8). **Animal side:** strong. **Audience:** medium to high (grazing patterns, diet) |
| **C. The rabbit gut as it is, including re-eating soft droppings** | No known animal of this size does this; it's a **bend** (HWC-16). Also a sensitive one for audience reception |

**Sub-choices and knock-ons.**
- **Ever-growing teeth plus soft or processed food** means chronic dental disease (pHWC-9).
- **Portable piece:** babies learning food preferences from what the mother ate, through the womb and milk. It works
  with any gut.
- **Rabbit-side toxins:** avocado (in the recorded case, the leaves) and the onion family, if the relevant genes come
  along.

**Can differ by lineage:** yes.

## CD-10 · Scent and body signals: the channels that run alongside language

**The question.** The base animal marks objects with a chin gland, carries a scent that says who it is and which group
it belongs to, and has rank written into its scent chemistry (dominants' marks last longer). It uses dung piles as
message boards and sprays urine in courtship and fights. In one study, group members smeared with a stranger's scent
were attacked by their own groupmates. **None of this is under voluntary control.**

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Keep the full scent system** | Identity and rank are broadcast whether they like it or not. Settlements might drift into local scent differences. Whether lineages would react to each other's identity scents is untested. **Animal side:** strong. **Audience:** medium (shown through marking and sniffing behavior and reactions), and dung and urine marking is sensitive |
| **B. Keep the identity and marking glands, drop dung and urine marking** | These rest on different glands, so they are separable in design. **Animal side:** strong to partial. **Audience:** medium |
| **C. No scent-signal system** | **Animal side:** none here |

**Related signals decided elsewhere:** tail-flash (CD-6), foot-thump and scream (CD-4), ear postures (CD-7), babies'
distress calls (CD-4; they work in the base). **Can differ by lineage:** yes.

## CD-12 · Can they have children with humans? (Q-31)

**The concrete question.** A rabbit-person and a human fall in love. Can they have a baby together?

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. No.** This is biology's answer | The two genomes are about 90 million years apart. Real mammals stop being able to hybridize after about 2–4 million years. Romance storylines stay; shared biological children don't. **No handwave** |
| **B. Yes, rarely and with difficulty, for one specific lineage** built on a human chromosome framework | A **bend** (HWC-9). Expect low fertility, sons most often sterile, and doubtful fertility in the children. That lineage becomes genetically closer to humans and less able to breed with other rabbit lineages (CD-14). Binds human canon |
| **C. Only with technology** (an artificial womb plus engineering) | Not natural interfertility: each such child is a new engineered person, made with the same technology that made the first generation (C-52, artificial wombs; C-37, designer babies). No new biology bend. The child's own fertility depends on which genome design it's given |

**Animal side:** no direct effect. **Can differ by lineage:** B is lineage-specific by its nature.

## CD-13 · How was the first generation carried and raised? (Q-32)

**The concrete question.** No unmodified human or rabbit mother could carry the first babies: the mother's immune
system rejects the pregnancy, and a rabbit can't carry a human-size baby. So how were they carried, and who raised
them?

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A. Artificial wombs** (established Tepenian technology, C-52) | Whatever flavors the fetus learns in the womb are whatever the device supplies. If rabbit newborn reflexes are kept (CD-3), the mother's milk scent must be supplied or the babies may not find the nipple (pHWC-7). There's no birth mother, so the human "saw my mother care for this baby" sibling cue has nothing to act on |
| **B. Engineered surrogate mothers** | The surrogate must be immune-tolerant and big enough, since the mother's size limits the baby's. Her milk, scent, and care become part of the first generation's start. Whether she could nurse them is unknown |
| **C. Another creator technology** (for example, an alien method) | Unknown. For alien-made lineages this also raises Q-17 (where the Earth DNA came from) |

**For every option:**
- **The infancy window.** If CD-4 keeps it, whoever cares for the babies in their first weeks becomes a "not scary"
  category for life, and predators are still feared. That could be human staff, robot caretakers, or older members.
  Creator type acts here only through the upbringing plan, never as an essence (rule 3).
- **Cohort rearing.** Raising the first generation in cohorts affects later mate choice (the co-rearing effect behind
  incest avoidance; L0 §6), on this race's own timescale.
- **The upbringing plan is the main hinge of the inherited culture (L3).**

**Can differ by lineage:** yes (HWC-7 needs an answer per lineage).

## Quick reference: which options keep the animal side (D-7)

| Decision | Options with a **strong** animal side | **Partial** | Most readable on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| CD-2 Pace | A | B | A (families, generations) |
| CD-3 Reproduction | B | C | B (sensitive) |
| CD-4 Fear and alarm | A, B | — | A, B: very high |
| CD-6 Body shape | — | B | B |
| CD-7 Head and senses | B | A, C | B: very high |
| CD-8 Diet and gut | B (C is a bend) | — | B |
| CD-9 Fur and heat | — | B | B |
| CD-10 Scent signals | A | B | A, B |
| CD-11 Clock | B | C | B |
| CD-16 Later breeding | — | — | B *erodes* the animal side over time |

**One pattern to know.** The rabbit traits that depend on its small body (breeding rate, re-eating droppings, the
minutes-a-day nursing, ear cooling) are the ones biology removes or weakens at human size. The traits nothing ties to
size are in the nervous system and senses: the fear system, horizon-scanning attention and vigilance, scent signaling,
the infancy window, inherited food preferences, and the night clock. **At human size, the animal side will mostly come
from those.**
