# Alpha Centauri System (RQ-9)

**Status:** Working source reference, 2026-10-21. **Real astronomy only.** Answers RQ-9 · Alpha Centauri system
for M-5 · Xenobiology First-Principles Method, Step 1 (stellar environment). Under **D-6 · Research the real
Alpha Centauri system**, published data on the real system is in scope. Under **D-2 · Locations hazy only**, no
settled planet, habitat, climate, or geography is chosen or built here. Nothing in this file is canon, and nothing
here says which body (if any) the *New Centauri* setting uses (C-19, C-23).

**Grounding tiers (M-5).** Every claim carries one:
- `GROUNDED`: a published measurement or model result, cited.
- `INFERRED`: a calculation or reasoned step from cited data, with the chain shown.
- `SPECULATIVE`: no firm basis. There are almost none here, and each is flagged.

**Confirmation vocabulary used below.**
- **Confirmed:** independently recovered or confirmed in the peer-reviewed literature.
- **Candidate:** reported but not confirmed.
- **Disputed:** reported, then challenged by a later analysis, and not resolved.
- **Retracted/refuted:** shown to be an artifact.

Exoplanet status changes. Every status below is "as of 2026-10-21, per the sources found," and M-5 Step 1 must
re-check it before use.

**Paraphrase only.** Quotes are short (under ~25 words). Numbers come from abstracts, the paper bodies that were
read, or are computed here (marked `INFERRED`).

**The masses are disputed at the ~3–5% level.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S01 Table 8 sets three solutions side by side:
- Akeson et al. 2021: A = 1.0788, B = 0.9092 M☉.
- Kervella et al. 2016: A = 1.1055, B = 0.9373 M☉.
- Pourbaix & Boffin 2016: A = 1.133, B = 0.972 M☉.

The difference comes mainly from the parallax adopted. AC-S01 is the most recent and most precise, but its own
authors expect further ALMA refinement.

**Age is genuinely uncertain.** `GROUNDED`. Published estimates span roughly 5 to 9 Gyr:
- 5.3 ± 0.3 Gyr (AC-S05, 2018), on masses ~3% higher than AC-S01's.
- 5 to 7 Gyr (adopted in AC-S02).
- About 6 to 6.5 Gyr (various asteroseismic models, 2003–2008; secondary).
- 7.2 to 7.8 Gyr preferred, and up to 8.7 ± 0.6 Gyr for low solar Z/X (AC-S04, 2026).

AC-S01 notes that the masses and the mixing-length treatment drive the spread. The firm statements are that the
system is **older than the Sun** (4.6 Gyr) in nearly all estimates, and **coeval** (AC-S02).
- `INFERRED`: any M-5 timeline that needs "how long has life had" should carry the full range, not a single value.

**Activity cycles.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S38.
- **B:** an X-ray cycle of about 8.2 ± 0.2 yr, about half the Sun's amplitude.
- **A:** reported as ~19 yr, but the record is short. A was in a long low state after 2005, and an earlier
  estimate said ≥15 yr. The period for A is **provisional**.

## Habitable zones

**Method.** `INFERRED`. The Kopparapu et al. (2014) analytic HZ fluxes (AC-S24) were computed using the author's
own coefficients:
- Seff = Seff☉ + a·T + b·T² + c·T³ + d·T⁴, with T = Teff − 5780 K.
- d = √(L/Seff).

Inputs: L from AC-S01 (A, B) and AC-S07 (Proxima), with Teff bracketed across the literature spread. The results
change by less than 1% across that bracket.

| Star | Optimistic inner (Recent Venus) | Conservative inner (Runaway Greenhouse, 1 M⊕) | Conservative outer (Maximum Greenhouse) | Optimistic outer (Early Mars) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| α Cen A (L = 1.506, Teff 5750–5800 K) | 0.92 au | 1.17 au | 2.06 au | 2.17 au |
| α Cen B (L = 0.498, Teff 5150–5320 K) | 0.55 au | 0.69 au | 1.24–1.25 au | 1.31–1.32 au |
| Proxima (L = 0.00151–0.00157, Teff 2900–3050 K) | 0.032 au | 0.041 au | 0.081–0.082 au | 0.085–0.086 au |

**Cross-checks.**
- **A:** AC-S01's introduction independently puts the center of A's HZ at ~1.2 au. `GROUNDED`
- **Proxima b:** S/S⊕ = L/a² = 0.00151/0.0485² ≈ **0.64** times Earth's instellation. This sits inside the
  conservative HZ. AC-S07 independently gives 877 ± 44 W/m², i.e. 64 ± 3% of the solar constant. `INFERRED`,
  matching `GROUNDED`
- **Proxima d:** S ≈ 1.8 S⊕ at 0.029 au, **inside the inner edge**, so it is not in the HZ. `INFERRED`
- **S1 candidate:** at ~2 au it lies within A's computed HZ, which is why AC-S16's title says "in the habitable
  zone." `INFERRED`. But it is a gas-giant candidate.

**Caveats.** `INFERRED`
- HZ edges are a property of a *model Earth-like planet* (an N₂–CO₂–H₂O atmosphere, 1 M⊕). AC-S24's 2014 update
  shows the inner edge moves with planet mass.
- In the AB pair, the companion adds a small, time-varying flux. At 11–35 au, B's light at A's HZ is tiny compared
  with A's.
- AC-S37 finds the companion's added XUV is unlikely to drive significant extra atmospheric loss for a planet
  around A. `GROUNDED`.

## Dynamical stability in the A–B binary

- **Circumstellar (S-type) orbits are stable only close in.** `GROUNDED`
  - AC-S22 (1997): test particles are unstable beyond roughly 0.2 binary semi-major axes from a star, and highly
    inclined orbits (~90°) are unstable even very close in.
  - AC-S21 (2016): billion-year stability for prograde, coplanar, circular orbits out to **~2.56 au around A**
    (vs. 2.34 au in AC-S22).
- **B's limit is slightly smaller.** `INFERRED` · from AC-S21's own fit. AC-S21 says B's limit is "slightly
  smaller" than A's. Its eccentricity fit gives about **2.4 au**, which is this file's calculation, not a number the
  paper prints.
- **Structure within the stable zone.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S21. Mean-motion resonances with the binary carve the
  stability edge. Retrograde orbits are stable over a wider zone. Lidov–Kozai cycling affects inclinations between
  ~39° and ~141°, and a broad region between ~75° and 105° is unstable.
- **The habitable zones of both A and B lie inside the stable zones.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S21's conclusion, and the
  computed HZs above (outer edges ~2.2 au for A and ~1.3 au for B).
- **Multi-planet systems.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S21 abstract, and the 2018 follow-up per search. These need wider
  spacing than around single stars to survive for billions of years.
- **Circumbinary (P-type) orbits around AB.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S22. Unstable inside roughly 3× the binary
  semi-major axis (~70 au).
- **Formation is harder than stability.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S23, and **disputed**.
  - Thébault et al. found planetesimal accretion only marginally possible (~0.5 au) in B's HZ, because the
    companion's perturbations raise collision speeds.
  - Xie et al. (2010) argue growth is possible once collisional evolution and binary inclination are included.
  - The S1 candidate itself, if real, sits on an orbit inclined to the binary plane (AC-S16).

## Radiation and flare environment

### Proxima (the dominant hazard in the system)

- **Quiescent high-energy load at Proxima b's orbit.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S25.
  - About 30× Earth's EUV and about 250× Earth's X-rays.
  - The time-averaged XUV, flares included, is ~0.29 W/m², roughly 60× Earth's (AC-S07).
  - The lifetime XUV dose is high. Per the search summary of the same group's follow-up, it is ~8–25× Earth's;
    that range was not re-fetched.
- **Flare rate.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S28. TESS recorded 72 flares in ~50 days (~1.5 per day, 7.2% of the time
  flaring). Extrapolated, that gives:
  - ~10³³ erg superflares about 3 times a year;
  - ~10³⁴ erg events roughly every two years.
- **A naked-eye superflare.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S27. A March 2016 superflare of ~10^33.5 erg (bolometric) made
  Proxima briefly visible to the naked eye. The flare rate implied at least ~5 superflares per year.
  - The authors' photochemical model (which **assumes** an Earth-like atmosphere) gives up to ~90% ozone loss
    within 5 years.
  - Their model also gives surface UV during such a flare of ~100× the dose lethal to UV-hardy microbes.
  - *These are model outcomes for a hypothetical atmosphere, not measurements of any planet.*
- **Extreme short flare.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S29. On 1 May 2019, multiwavelength coverage caught a flare lasting under
  10 s, with a far-UV brightening of more than 14,000× and a millimeter brightening of more than 1,000×.
- **Coronal mass ejections.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S30. A flare-associated type IV radio burst (2020) is the kind of
  signal that, on the Sun, follows CMEs.
  - **This is disputed as proof of a CME.** Outside commentators note that a CME is not guaranteed.
  - AC-S31 mentions "possible indications" of two CMEs.
- **Activity cycle.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S31. An ~8-yr cycle in X-ray, UV, and optical. Proxima is the only fully
  convective star with long-term X-ray cycle monitoring.
- **Early history.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S25. Before reaching the HZ, about 100–200 Myr after formation (the
  pre-main-sequence phase), Proxima b may have passed through a runaway-greenhouse phase. It may have lost up to
  about an Earth ocean of hydrogen. The authors call the initial water budget the largest uncertainty.

### Tidal locking (Proxima b)

- **Spin state.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S25. Proxima b's spin is expected to be **synchronous or in a 3:2 spin–orbit
  resonance**, depending on eccentricity and triaxiality, with near-zero obliquity. Which of the two applies is
  **not determined**.
- **Climate under each spin state.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S26 (3D climate models).
  - With enough water and a synchronous spin, liquid water persists at least near the substellar point.
  - The 3:2 case needs modest greenhouse warming (on the order of ~10 mbar CO₂ with 1 bar N₂).
  - Drier planets risk cold-trapping their water on the nightside or at the poles unless CO₂ is ~0.5–1.5 bar.
  - *These are model scenarios conditional on an assumed atmosphere.*

### Atmospheric retention: the open debate

- **Proxima b is not observed to have an atmosphere.** `GROUNDED`
  - It does not transit (no transit found in TESS, AC-S28).
  - Thermal phase curves were proposed as the test (AC-S39).
  - **No JWST result on Proxima b was found as of 2026-10-21.**
- **The pessimistic case.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S32. Model escape for an Earth-like atmosphere is up to ~10,000× Earth's
  rate. The press summary says that even an Earth-like magnetic field leaves polar escape paths. The authors call
  this a simple, average-activity model.
- **The cosmic shoreline.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S33, AC-S34. The shoreline sorts bodies that keep air by escape velocity
  against cumulative irradiation and XUV. AC-S34 finds historic XUV for planets of mid-to-late M dwarfs is 2.1–3.1×
  the canonical scaling. Within that framework, only the largest known terrestrial planets of such stars likely
  kept atmospheres.
  - `INFERRED`: Proxima (M5.5V) is a mid-to-late M dwarf. Proxima b is near Earth-mass, not among the "largest
    terrestrial" planets, so the framework leans against retention. The framework itself is not settled (AC-S36:
    it is still being tested statistically).
- **Empirical analogs.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S35. JWST found no thick atmospheres on TRAPPIST-1 b and c, both rocky
  planets of a late M dwarf. Surface pressures above ~1 bar are strongly disfavored, and TRAPPIST-1 c may still
  have a thin O₂-rich atmosphere.
  - `INFERRED`: these are a different star and different planets. They are analogs, not evidence about Proxima b.
- **The optimistic counterpoint.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S25. Proxima b remains "a viable candidate habitable planet," and
  outgassing, a large initial volatile inventory, or a magnetic field could change the outcome.
- **Bottom line.** `INFERRED`. The question is **open**. The weight of recent work tilts toward "thin or no
  atmosphere is plausible," but nothing measured on Proxima b settles it.

### A and B

- **Activity.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S37, AC-S38. Sun-like, low-to-moderate activity. A is slightly hotter and brighter
  than the Sun, and B is cooler.
- **Binarity and XUV.** `GROUNDED` · AC-S37. A companion star's added XUV is unlikely to cause significant extra
  atmospheric loss for Earth-like planets around A.
- **Spectrum available to life.** `INFERRED` · from the luminosities and temperatures above.
  - Around A, close to solar.
  - Around B, somewhat redder (K1V).
  - Around Proxima, strongly shifted to the red and near-IR, with little visible light. AC-S07 describes the
    spectrum as "much redder" than the Sun's.

## What this does and does not settle for M-5

**It does settle the following for M-5 Step 1** (`GROUNDED`):
- the three stars' types, luminosities, and spectral character;
- that all three are one bound, coeval, metal-rich system older than the Sun;
- the HZ distances for each star;
- the binary geometry, and the ~2.4–2.6 au stability limits around A and B;
- that Proxima is a frequently flaring, high-XUV M5.5 dwarf;
- which planets are confirmed (Proxima b and d), disputed (Proxima c), candidate (α Cen A S1/C1), or refuted (α Cen Bb).

**It does not settle anything in Step 2.**
- No planet's atmosphere, surface, water, rotation (for Proxima b, two options), or habitability has been measured.
- The real, confirmed planets give M-5 **bounds for `[hazy]` archetype branches**:
  - a red-dwarf branch: a rocky planet in an M5.5 dwarf's HZ, possibly synchronous, with a high flare and XUV load
    and retention in doubt;
  - a Sun-like branch: an undetected planet inside the ~2.5 au stable zone of a G2 or K1 star, with a distant
    companion star.
- These branches are not choices. Under D-2/D-6, whether the fiction uses a real body, a hypothetical one, or an
  engineered habitat is a developer ruling. Canon says only "the Centauri binary star system" (C-19) and
  "inhabited, landable planets" (C-23), with no body named.
- The exoplanet facts here will age. Re-check the statuses (especially S1 and Proxima c) before any use.
